## SPEAKING LONGER THAN LEARNED: TRAINING-FREE LONG-FORM GENERATION FOR FULL-STREAM TEXT-TO-SPEECH

ABSTRACT 

Recent full-stream text-to-speech (TTS) models enable low-latency personalized synthesis as input text arrives. However, sustaining generation beyond the lengths seen during train-ing without additional training remains largely unexplored. We systematically analyze VoXtream2, which extends gen-eration by periodically discarding past context while retain-ing the acoustic prompt. We find that this strategy does not prevent degradation: pronunciation accuracy deteriorates near the end of the training range due to the range of positions in which the model operates rather than the loss of past context, while speaker similarity gradually declines throughout gener-ation. Based on these findings, we propose two training-free modifications that reduce the attention window and strengthen attention to the acoustic prompt. Experiments show that these modifications preserve pronunciation accuracy and speaker similarity throughout long-form generation while retaining the naturalness of the original model. Ablations further show complementary effects of the two modifications. 1

Index Terms — streaming text-to-speech (TTS), long-form TTS, training-free length extrapolation, attention bias 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Recent text-to-speech (TTS) systems support zero-shot voice cloning and expressive speech generation [1], while growing demand for spoken conversational systems has increased in-terest in streaming TTS. Recent approaches enable synthe-sis before the complete input text is available through inter-leaved speech-text modeling [2], direct autoregressive gener-ation from incoming text [3], or streaming acoustic decod-ing [1]. These advances have made low-latency TTS increas-ingly practical for real-time conversational systems. Beyond short utterances, many applications require long-form synthesis over multiple sentences or paragraphs [4]. Concatenating independently synthesized segments can in-troduce speaker inconsistencies and boundary artifacts [5], while long-form paired speech data remains scarce. Con-sequently, synthesis beyond the sequence lengths observed

Sim-ilar challenges have been studied in large language models (LLMs) through additional long-context training or training-free context management [6]. StreamingLLM [6] retains a few initial attention sink tokens together with a bounded win-dow of recent tokens to enable inference beyond the training context. Extending generation beyond the sequence lengths seen during training has also been explored in long-form TTS. Ap-proaches based on additional training improve length extrapo-lation through positional modeling or alignment, while others explicitly train on long-duration speech for long-form gener-ation [4]. Training-free approaches instead avoid additional long-form training; MagpieTTS-LF [5] extends TTS models through stateful chunk generation and attention priors. More recently, long-form synthesis with streaming text input has also been explored, although existing approaches require ad-ditional training to operate with a bounded streaming con-text [7]. To the best of our knowledge, training-free length extrapolation for a pretrained full-stream TTS model under continuous long-form generation has not been explored. In this work, we tackle training-free length extrapolation in continuous long-form generation using VoXtream2 [3], whose official implementation extends generation beyond the training length by periodically resetting the key-value cache while retaining the acoustic prompt and recent frames, following the attention sink concept [6]. However, this mech-anism alone cannot sustain long-form generation: pronunci-ation accuracy degrades near the end of the training range, while speaker similarity declines gradually throughout the utterance. We find that the former stems from the positions at which the model operates rather than from the cache reset itself, while the latter persists even when these positions are avoided. Based on these findings, we shrink the attention win-dow to keep the model within positions encountered during training and bias attention toward the acoustic prompt, the only intact explicit speaker reference in the context. We first show that the loss of speaker similarity is not unique to VoXtream2, but also occurs in open-source TTS models trained on long-form speech. Applied to VoXtream2, our methods preserve both pronunciation accuracy and speaker similarity throughout long-form generation with streaming arXiv:2610.19553v1 [cs.SD] 14 Oct 2026 text input. Human evaluation confirms comparable natural-ness, while ablations on attention window size and bias design show complementary effects on long-form stability. 

2. METHOD 2.1. VoXtream2 

VoXtream2 [3] is a full-stream TTS model conditioned on ref-erence speech, which is encoded into Mimi codec tokens [8] and serves as an acoustic prompt. It consists of a Phoneme Transformer (PT), a Temporal Transformer (TT), and a Depth Transformer (DT). PT incrementally encodes phonemes, TT autoregressively predicts semantic and duration tokens from these embeddings and previous audio tokens, and DT gener-ates the remaining codebooks. Each duration token combines a shift state s ∈ { 0, 1, 2}, indicating the base phoneme ad-vancement, with a binary phoneme-count state u ∈ { 0, 1},where u = 0 and u = 1 correspond to assigning one and two phonemes to the current frame. The effective phoneme advancement per frame is s + u ∈ { 0, 1, 2, 3}. VoXtream2 is trained on random 50 s crops ( 625 frames at 12 .5Hz). During long-form generation, the key-value (KV) cache keeps growing and must be bounded. StreamingLLM [6] showed that a bounded cache can remain stable by retaining the first few tokens, known as attention sinks, together with the most recent ones. The official VoXtream2 implementation follows a similar idea in its SinkAttention module. TT indexes cached frames by local position and holds at most W

frames, where audio window size sets W = 625 by de-fault. Once the local position reaches W , a cache reset retains the P acoustic prompt frames and the τ = ⌊W/ 2⌋ recent generated frames while discarding the rest. The frames are re-indexed from 0 and their KV states recomputed, so gener-ation resumes at local position P + τ . The local position then climbs back to W , triggering the next reset after W − P − τ

frames and cycling between P + τ and W .

2.2.1. Position, Not Cache Reset 

Training crops are 625 frames long, so TT never undergoes a cache reset during training and observes local positions above roughly 600 only near the end of a crop, when little of the transcript remains. With the default W = 625 , however, the local position repeatedly enters this region immediately be-fore each cache reset during long-form generation. The sharp WER increase in Figure 1 may therefore arise either from position-dependent behavior near the end of the training range or from the cache reset itself. 2.2.2. Attending to the Prompt 

Unlike the sharp increase in pronunciation errors above, SPK-SIM declines steadily throughout generation (Figure 1). Reducing W does not remove this trend but slows it, from 

−0.082 per 100 s with W = 625 to −0.054 with W = 400 .Nor does the decline track reset frequency: with W = 400 ,resets are more frequent than with W = 625 , yet SPK-SIM degrades more slowly. We therefore examine whether the acoustic prompt remains important throughout generation. To test this hypothesis, after 30s, we prevent the model from attending to the acoustic prompt keys while retaining the generated-history cache. With W = 400 , the cache con-tains at least the most recent τ = ⌊W/ 2⌋ = 200 generated frames, corresponding to 16 s of speech. Despite this context, SPK-SIM drops from 0.73 to 0.23 in the next 10 s window and remains below 0.25 thereafter (Figure 3). This indicates that the acoustic prompt remains essential throughout gener-ation, but its P frames remain fixed while generated frames continue to accumulate in the cache. 3. EXPERIMENTS 3.1. Experimental Setups 

All VoXtream2-based [3] experiments use the official imple-mentation and pretrained checkpoint. Our method requires no additional training. We follow the same LibriSpeech-Long [4] evaluation protocol and objective metrics as in Section 2.2. In addition to VoXtream2 and ours, we compare against Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base [12] and MOSS-TTS-v1.5 [13], recent TTS models capable of long-form synthesis. Both baselines use long-form training, whereas ours directly extends pre-trained VoXtream2 without additional training. All models receive the same acoustic prompt and target text; when required, the corresponding reference text is also provided. We further evaluate Mimi codec reconstruction [8] to quantify degradation introduced by encoding and decoding. All audio is resampled to 16kHz for evaluation. In addition to WER and SPK-SIM, we evaluate naturalness and overall speech quality using mean opinion score (MOS). For MOS evaluation, 158 listeners rate overall naturalness and audio quality on a five-point scale, yielding 325 ratings per model.

Our method achieves the same MOS of 4.03 as VoXtream2, close to the Mimi reconstruction upper bound of 4.10, indicating that the improved long-form robustness does not compromise perceptual quality. Interestingly, speaker similarity degradation with gener-ation length is not unique to VoXtream2. Qwen3-TTS and MOSS-TTS, despite being trained with long-form data, also exhibit decreasing SPK-SIM toward later segments. Qwen3-TTS, for example, drops from 0.780 to 0.560, whereas our method remains substantially more stable, from 0.770 to 0.754. This suggests that maintaining speaker identity over extended generation remains a broader challenge even for models explicitly trained for long-form synthesis.

Table 2 analyzes context window restriction and acoustic prompt attention bias. Reducing the Temporal Transformer window from W = 625 improves WER, with W = 375 

achieving the best value of 4.82, whereas further reducing it to W = 250 degrades performance. Despite more frequent cache resets, W = 375 remains real-time on an NVIDIA RTX 3090, with real-time factor (RTF) increasing only from 0.415 at W = 625 to 0.433. With W = 375 , increasing the constant bias improves speaker similarity; b = 2 .0 achieves the highest SPK-SIM of 0.766, while b = 1.5 yields the lowest WER of 3.58 with a comparable SPK-SIM of 0.759. SpotLight-based adaptive steering [15] improves WER over window restriction alone but remains less effective than con-stant bias for speaker similarity. We therefore use W = 375 

and a constant bias of b = 1 .5 for our experiments. 

4. CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we proposed a training-free approach for long-form generation with full-stream TTS, based on a systematic analysis of VoXtream2. By shrinking the attention window to keep the model within the positions encountered during train-ing and applying a bias to the attention logits of the acous-tic prompt, we preserved both pronunciation accuracy and speaker similarity throughout long-form generation while re-taining the naturalness of the original model. 5.
