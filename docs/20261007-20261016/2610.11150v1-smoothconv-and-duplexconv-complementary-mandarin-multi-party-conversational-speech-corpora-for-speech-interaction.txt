ABSTRACT 

Recent advances in large audio language models (LALMs) have driven the development of natural and intelligent speech interac-tion systems. Such systems need to model complex conversational behaviors, including turn-taking, overlapping speech, and speaker coordination. Multi-party conversations provide a realistic setting for studying these behaviors, yet existing Mandarin conversational corpora often lack synchronized participant-level speech tracks and comprehensive annotations. In this work, we introduce Smooth-Conv and DuplexConv , two complementary Mandarin multi-party conversational speech corpora totaling 2,100 hours. SmoothConv provides human-verified conversations for reliable analysis and eval-uation, while DuplexConv offers large-scale automatically annotated conversations through a scalable pipeline for model training. Both corpora provide synchronized participant-level speech tracks and multi-dimensional fine-grained annotations. We further release the 

SmoothConv Benchmark and evaluate these resources on speech separation, multi-speaker automatic speech recognition (MSASR), and turn detection tasks. Experimental results demonstrate the utility of the proposed resources for multi-party speech interaction mod-eling. The datasets, benchmark, and related resources are publicly available. 1

Index Terms — Multi-party conversational speech, speech inter-action, speech corpus, benchmark 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Recent advances in large audio language models (LALMs) have made natural, real-time, and coherent speech interaction an in-creasingly important area. Beyond recognizing speech content and speaker identity, interactive speech systems need to understand con-versational behaviors, including turn-taking, response timing, and paralinguistic cues. Multi-party conversations provide a natural setting for studying such behaviors, as multiple speakers coordinate their speech through turn exchanges, overlaps, and evolving inter-action states. However, existing conversational resources often lack either synchronized participant-level speech tracks or comprehen-sive annotations of interaction behaviors, limiting their applicability to realistic multi-party speech interaction. Switchboard [1] and Fisher [2] contain nat-ural conversations with separate speaker channels, but are limited to English two-speaker telephone conversations. CHiME-5 [3] cap-tures realistic multi-speaker conversations in everyday environments using multi-channel recordings, but mainly targets robust ASR and speech separation without comprehensive fine-grained annotations. AMI [4] and AliMeeting [5] provide multi-party meeting recordings with speaker annotations, while AISHELL-4 [6] introduces Man-darin multi-party meeting data. However, these resources either rely on microphone array recordings or lack synchronized participant-level speech tracks and detailed annotations of interaction behaviors. Consequently, publicly available Mandarin resources remain insuffi-cient for modeling realistic multi-party conversations. Another challenge lies in scaling multi-dimensional annotation to large conversational datasets. While many conversational corpora provide basic speech transcriptions and speaker information, fine-grained descriptions of interaction behaviors and speaker attributes remain limited. Manual annotation provides reliable and detailed labels, but is costly and difficult to extend to large-scale data. Au-tomatic annotation improves scalability, yet may introduce errors in recognition, alignment, and semantic interpretation. This motivates the development of Mandarin conversational resources that combine arXiv:2610.11150v1 [eess.AS] 8 Oct 2026 carefully verified data for reliable analysis and automatically con-structed data for scalable model training. To address these limitations, we introduce SmoothConv and 

DuplexConv , two complementary Mandarin multi-party conversa-tional speech corpora totaling 2,100 hours across Tutoring and So-cial Chat scenarios. Both corpora provide synchronized participant-level speech tracks and multi-dimensional annotations while serv-ing different research purposes. SmoothConv contains 100 hours of naturally occurring conversations with detailed human verifica-tion, supporting fine-grained analysis and reliable evaluation. Du-plexConv contains 2,000 hours of automatically annotated conver-sations generated through a scalable pipeline, supporting large-scale model training. We further release the SmoothConv Benchmark ,including evaluation data and protocols for reproducible multi-party speech interaction research. We evaluate these resources on three representative tasks spanning different aspects of multi-party speech interaction, including speech separation for participant-level signal recovery, multi-speaker automatic speech recognition (MSASR) for speaker-aware content understanding, and turn detection for con-versational behavior modeling. The results demonstrate the util-ity of these resources for training and evaluating models on multi-party conversational speech, highlighting the complementary value of large-scale automatically annotated data and high-quality human-verified annotations. 

2. DATASET 

SmoothConv and DuplexConv are built from synchronized multi-party conversations collected from real-world online interactions. Unlike scripted, predefined-topic, or role-playing recordings, these sessions preserve spontaneous conversational behaviors, including turn exchanges, interruptions, speech overlaps, and dynamic inter-action patterns. All recordings were collected with informed con-sent and anonymized before release, with no personally identifiable information retained. SmoothConv emphasizes annotation quality through human ver-ification and supports reliable analysis and evaluation, whereas Du-plexConv adopts a scalable automatic annotation pipeline to provide large-scale training data. 

2.1. Construction Pipeline 

As shown in Fig. 1, the pipeline consists of two stages: per-channel recognition and speaker labeling, followed by multi-dimensional an-notation. 

Per-channel recognition and speaker labeling. For each participant track, VAD first detects speech regions while pre-serving the original timeline synchronization. Multiple ASR systems [10, 11, 12, 13] independently generate transcripts, and ROVER [14] fusion combines their outputs into a unified recog-nition result. Dasheng [15] is then applied to remove non-speech segments. Pyannote diarization [16] and Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B [17] further refine speaker attribution and utterance boundaries. Finally, 3D-Speaker clustering [18] provides consistent speaker la-bels across the complete conversation. 

Multi-dimensional fine-grained annotation. Based on aligned transcripts and speaker labels, we construct annotations for con-versational turn states, paralinguistic information, and speaker attributes. To evaluate the quality of automatic annotations, we randomly sampled a 20-hour subset from DuplexConv for human verification. The sampled data were independently reviewed by the same annotator team involved in SmoothConv annotation. A sample is considered valid when the automatically generated annotations satisfy the predefined quality criteria covering transcription, speaker labeling, and conversational interaction annotations. The human verification pass rate on the sampled subset is 82.4%, indicating that the proposed automatic annotation pipeline achieves practical annotation quality for large-scale corpus construction. For turn-state annotations specifically, each turn is assigned one of four states: complete , indicating a semantically finished utter-ance; incomplete , indicating an unfinished or interrupted utterance; 

backchannel , representing brief acknowledgment or feedback; and 

wait , representing a request to pause or hold the conversation [19, 20]. Qwen3-32B [21] first predicts candidate turn states from textual context, and Qwen3-Omni [22] subsequently incorporates acoustic information for verification. On the expert-reviewed 20-hour sub-set, the automatically generated turn-state labels achieve 91% agree-ment with human annotations. When predictions disagree, the audio-based decision is preferred for incomplete turns and backchannels, where prosodic cues are particularly important. Emotion, nonverbal events, gender, and age attributes are auto-matically annotated using Gemini 2.5 Pro [23]. Age attributes are categorized into five groups in DuplexConv: child, teenager, adult, middle-aged, and elderly. Paralinguistic annotations describe emo-tional states and nonverbal vocal events, including crying, coughing, throat clearing, sneezing, sighing, and other vocal behaviors beyond lexical content. SmoothConv adopts predefined emotion categories to facilitate annotation and quality control, including neutral, happy, confused, curious, angry, surprise, sad, contempt, worry, disgust, fear, and sorry. In contrast, DuplexConv uses free-form emotion de-scriptions to represent diverse emotional expressions. 

2.2. Dataset Statistics 

The released corpora contain Mandarin conversations from two sce-narios: Tutoring and Social Chat. Each session includes two to four synchronized participant-level speech tracks, preserving natu-ral speech overlap and long-range conversational context. 

SmoothConv is a human-verified corpus containing 100 hours from 2,503 conversation clips. It covers natural two- to four-speaker conversations with diverse interaction patterns. Each conversation is annotated by four annotators and further reviewed by three senior annotators, covering transcripts, speaker identities, utterance bound-aries, turn states, and interaction attributes. SmoothConv provides reliable supervision for fine-grained analysis and evaluation of multi-party speech interaction. DuplexConv contains 2,000 hours from 93,709 clips constructed through the automatic annotation pipeline. The average clip duration is 76.8 s, ranging from 8 s to 618 s. It includes conversations with two to four speakers and provides scalable automatically generated annotations to support large-scale model training.

3. EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate the proposed corpora on three downstream tasks closely related to multi-party speech interaction: speech separation, multi-speaker automatic speech recognition (MSASR), and conversational turn detection. These tasks cover three important aspects of in-teractive speech processing. Speech separation evaluates whether the corpus can support reliable speaker-level signal recovery, which serves as a prerequisite for subsequent speaker-aware interaction un-derstanding. MSASR focuses on understanding “who said what”, providing the fundamental content representation required for inter-action modeling. Turn detection further investigates conversational dynamics by modeling speaker transitions and interaction states. All training and evaluation splits are strictly separated, with no shared conversation sessions or speakers. When SmoothConv is used for training, the corresponding evaluation samples are removed to avoid data leakage.

3.2. Multi-speaker Automatic Speech Recognition 

For MSASR, we adapt the SoulX-Transcriber [30] model using con-versational data from DuplexConv and SmoothConv. LoRA [31] adaptation is applied while keeping the backbone model unchanged. All models are fine-tuned for one epoch with a learning rate of 1 ×

10 −5, a warmup ratio of 0.05, and LoRA parameters of rank 8 and 

α = 32 . All training and evaluation splits are strictly separated. Evaluation is conducted on the SmoothConv Benchmark and ex-ternal conversational datasets, including AliMeeting [5] and AMI-SDM [4]. DER, cpWER, and tcpWER are reported with a 0.25 s collar for diarization evaluation. As shown in Table 4, DuplexConv adaptation substantially improves MSASR performance on the SmoothConv Benchmark, reducing both diarization and transcription errors compared with the baseline. Combining DuplexConv with SmoothConv further improves performance under several evaluation conditions, demon-strating the complementary benefits of scalable automatically anno-tated data and human-verified annotations. These results indicate that the proposed corpora effectively support speaker-aware content understanding for multi-party speech interaction. The performance degradation on far-field AliMeeting further suggests that acous-tic mismatch remains a challenge for cross-domain conversational adaptation.

4. CONCLUSION 

In this work, we introduce SmoothConv and DuplexConv, two complementary Mandarin multi-party conversational corpora with participant-level tracks and multi-dimensional annotations. Smooth-Conv provides high-quality human-verified data for analysis and evaluation, while DuplexConv enables large-scale model training with automatically annotated conversations. We further release the SmoothConv Benchmark, a task-oriented evaluation suite covering different aspects of multi-party speech interaction. Experiments on speech separation, MSASR, and turn detection demonstrate the utility of these publicly available resources for modeling natural multi-party speech interaction. 5. REFERENCES
