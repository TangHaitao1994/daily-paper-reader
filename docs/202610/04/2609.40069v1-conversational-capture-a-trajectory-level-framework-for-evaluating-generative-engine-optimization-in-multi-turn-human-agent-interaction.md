## 摘要
生成式引擎优化（GEO）塑造内容，以提高其被构建于检索增强大语言模型之上的答案引擎引用的可能性。GEO通常被评估为一种单轮属性：对于固定查询，评估者测量某个来源在单次回答中的可见性。我们认为，单次回答是不充分的分析单元。人机信息寻求形成一个闭环：智能体的回答改变用户的信念，从而改变下一个问题，而下一个问题又决定了智能体检索什么。我们引入对话捕获这一现象，即早期被引用的来源变得显著更可能再次被引用。捕获通过机器侧通道——历史条件检索——和人类侧通道——针对被捕获来源的后续问题——发挥作用。我们将这种交互形式化为一个两层闭环系统，并推导出轨迹级构造：累积对话可见性；轨迹增益的直接/反馈分解；将反馈项嵌套拆分为机器侧和人类侧通道；捕获系数；复合比率；以及误排诊断。利用强化过程（波利亚瓮）理论，我们证明在单轮评估下反馈项为零，并且随着对话长度的增加，当捕获发展时，GEO的累积收益超线性增长。一个模型推导的示例表明，反馈项可以超过直接项，复合比率在十轮内超过二，并且单轮排名与轨迹排名仅弱一致（肯德尔τ=0.4）。我们将人类通道与信息觅食、信任校准和贝叶斯说服联系起来，并讨论了对答案引擎的设计启示。

## Abstract
Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) shapes content to increase its likelihood of being cited by answer engines built on retrieval-augmented large language models. GEO is typically evaluated as a single-turn property: for a fixed query, an evaluator measures a source's visibility in one answer. We argue that the single answer is an inadequate unit of analysis. Human-agent information seeking forms a closed loop: the agent's answer changes the user's beliefs and therefore the next question, which in turn determines what the agent retrieves. We introduce conversational capture, a phenomenon in which a source cited early becomes substantially more likely to be cited again. Capture operates through a machine-side channel, history-conditioned retrieval, and a human-side channel, follow-up questions directed toward the captured source. We formalize the interaction as a two-layer closed-loop system and derive trajectory-level constructs: cumulative conversational visibility; a direct/feedback decomposition of trajectory gain; a nested split of the feedback term into machine-side and human-side channels; a capture coefficient; a compounding ratio; and a misranking diagnostic. Using reinforcement-process (Pólya-urn) theory, we prove that the feedback term is zero under single-turn evaluation and that GEO's cumulative payoff grows superlinearly with conversation length while capture develops. A model-derived illustration shows that the feedback term can exceed the direct term, the compounding ratio exceeds two within ten turns, and single-turn and trajectory rankings agree only weakly (Kendall's $τ= 0.4$). We connect the human channel to information foraging, trust calibration, and Bayesian persuasion, and discuss design implications for answer engines.
