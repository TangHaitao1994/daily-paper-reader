# DuplexSpeechBench-Document Grounding : Benchmarking Document Grounding and Hallucinations in Voice Agents 

Puneet Mathur 1, Nedim Lipka 1, Zeyu Jin 1, Dinesh Manocha 2

> 1Adobe Research, San Jose, USA

Abstract 

Voice agents enable low-latency, natural interaction, yet their ability to faithfully ground responses in external documents remains underexplored. We introduce 

DuplexSpeechBench-Document Grounding 

(DSB-DG) , a benchmark for evaluating doc-ument grounding in voice agents across five professional domains. DSB-DG targets three failure modes: Context Saturation , which mea-sures grounding under increasing document length; Grounding Decay , which measures re-tention of document facts across multi-turn di-alogue; and Proactive Grounding , which eval-uates whether context re-injection mitigates conversational drift. The benchmark contains 1,636 adversarially verified QA pairs from 50 documents covering five professional do-mains, and supports fully automatic evaluation of grounding accuracy, hallucination, and re-sponse latency. Across systems spanning cas-caded, proprietary full-duplex and real-time, and open-weight speech2speech architectures, we find substantial differences in effective grounding capacity. While cascaded pipeline (ASR-LLM-TTS) achieves the highest ground-ing accuracy, Gemini-Live and GPT-Realtime closely trail behind. Open-weight systems ex-hibit distinct failure modes, most notably an abrupt context-capacity collapse and multi-turn grounding decay. More broadly, grounding fi-delity degrades with context and conversational load, and failures frequently manifest as unsup-ported generations rather than abstention. We show that contextual grounding as a key unre-solved challenge for reliable full-duplex voice agents.

2.1 Context Saturation 

Context Saturation measures grounding robustness as document length increases. By holding the ques-tion and supporting evidence fixed while expanding context, the protocol isolates the effect of contex-tual load and captures both gradual degradation and abrupt context-capacity collapse. 

Setting : For a source document D, we construct five prefix-consistent tiers T1 ⊂ T2 ⊂ · · · ⊂ T5

of approximately 500, 1k, 2k, 4k, and 8k tokens, such that information in Ti remains available in all subsequent tiers. For each question–answer pair (q, a ), we ask the same spoken question q

under every eligible tier Ti and obtain response ri.Interactions are single-turn with no conversational history, so changes in grounding can be attributed to increasing contextual load. 

2.2 Grounding Decay 

Context capacity alone does not capture whether document information remains accessible as a con-versation progresses. Grounding decay measures retention of document facts under controlled con-versational drift. 

Setting : For each document D, we construct a multi-turn conversation following a probe– distractor–repeat structure. Early turns contain document-grounded probes A, intermediate turns introduce domain-relevant distractors B, and late turns repeat the original probes as A′. As A′ is identical to A, performance changes reflect con-versational progression rather than question vari-ation. We define the Grounding Decay Score: 

GDS = GA( A′) − GA( A), where GA( ·) denotes grounding accuracy over the corresponding probe set. Negative values indicate grounding loss and values near zero indicate stable retention. GDS is interpreted jointly with GA( A), since near-zero decay is uninformative when initial grounding is poor. Distractors remain domain-related but do not require the probed facts, inducing conversational drift without changing the source context. Conver-sation templates are provided in Appendix E.2. 

2.3 Proactive Grounding 

Proactive Grounding evaluates whether refreshing document context mitigates grounding loss during extended dialogue. Additional context may im-prove access to source information, but may also overwhelm the effective conversational context of some speech LM architectures.

3 Benchmark Design 

Benchmark Composition. Table 2 shows DSB-DG comprises 50 professional documents across five domains: clinical trial protocols, earnings-call transcripts, FDA 510(k) filings, commercial lease agreements, and municipal meeting minutes. The benchmark contains 1,636 verified question– answer pairs and 20 controlled multi-turn conversa-tions. Each document is represented at five prefix-consistent context tiers of approximately 500, 1k, 2k, 4k, and 8k tokens, enabling controlled evalua-tion of context-length effects while preserving evi-dence from shorter tiers. Questions span entity and numerical retrieval, procedural reasoning, clause interpretation, and multi-hop document comprehen-sion. Additional dataset statistics in Appendix B. 

QA Generation and Verification. Candidate QA pairs are generated using GPT-OSS-20B paired with restricted document context to ensure the QA is blind to extraneous information, followed by three-stage adversarial verification. This multi-stage process results in 1,636 QA pairs (1,341 grounded and 295 unanswerable). We addition-ally construct controlled multi-turn conversations containing document-grounded probes, domain-relevant distractors, and topic transitions. See Ap-pendix B.5 for QA verification, Appendix B.4 for prompts, and Appendix E for protocol templates. 

Audio Interaction. All benchmark queries are synthesized as 16kHz mono speech using a fixed TTS voice, standardizing speaker, prosody, and recording conditions across systems. The bench-mark isolates document grounding under standard-ized acoustic input. TTS processing configurations are provided in Appendix D. 

LLM-Judge Evaluation. Model responses are transcribed with Whisper-large-v3 (Radford et al., 2023) and scored by an LLM judge given the source context, question, reference answer, and re-sponse transcript to determine if the responses are supported by the supplied document context. See Appendix C for judge evaluation configurations.

Capacity limits amplify hallucination. Ground-ing degradation is accompanied by increasing un-supported generation (Figure 3). SALMONN’s T3 →T4 accuracy collapse coincides with HR in-creasing from 30.1% to 39.8%, reaching 46.5% at T5. Thus, context-capacity failures often manifest as hallucination rather than abstention. 

Document domain also affects grounding. Ta-ble 4 shows that clinical trial protocols and FDA 510(k) filings are consistently the most challeng-ing due to their dense, specification-heavy content and need for fine-grained factual distinctions. 6 Conclusion 

We introduced DuplexSpeechBench-Document Grounding (DSB-DG) benchmark for evaluating document grounding in voice agents. We identify distinct failure modes under contextual and con-versational load: abrupt context-capacity collapse, multi-turn grounding decay, and increased hallu-cination as grounding deteriorates. Our findings show that conversational responsiveness alone is in-sufficient for reliable document-grounded voice in-teractions. DSB-DG provides a reproducible bench-mark for developing full-duplex voice agents that jointly optimize factual grounding and conversa-tional latency. 7 Limitations 

DSB-DG is designed to isolate document ground-ing under controlled spoken interaction, and sev-eral limitations define the scope of its conclusions. First, the benchmark covers five professional do-mains and contexts up to approximately 8k tokens; substantially longer documents, other domains, and less structured knowledge sources remain outside the present evaluation. Second, user questions are rendered using standardized synthetic speech. This controls acoustic variability across systems but does not measure robustness to speaker diversity, accents, background noise, spontaneous speech, or naturally occurring disfluencies. Third, the eval-uated architectures expose different mechanisms for conditioning on external text. We use each system’s closest available context interface, but these mechanisms are not architecturally equiva-lent. The results therefore characterize complete deployed systems rather than a controlled compar-ison of language-model backbones alone. Fourth, grounding quality is evaluated primarily with an LLM-based judge. The structured judge captures factual correctness and hallucination behavior at scale, but residual errors may remain for partially correct, implicitly supported, or acoustically am-biguous responses. Finally, DSB-DG focuses on factual grounding, conversational retention, con-text re-injection, and response latency rather than the complete capability profile of production voice agents. It does not directly evaluate naturalness, emotion, speaker similarity, interruption handling, safety, or task-completion quality. These dimen-sions are complementary to document grounding and should be considered jointly in real-world de-ployment. 

8 Ethics Statement 

DSB-DG is designed to evaluate document ground-ing in full-duplex speech systems without rely-ing on personal or sensitive user information. The benchmark does not contain private conversa-tions, personally identifying information, or person-specific factual queries, and is not intended to re-trieve or infer sensitive attributes about individuals. Documents are used solely as grounding sources for controlled evaluation, and benchmark questions target document content rather than personal pro-filing. All user speech is synthetically generated using a fixed TTS voice, avoiding the collection or release of human voice recordings and reducing pri-vacy risks associated with speaker identity. Model outputs may nevertheless contain hallucinated or unsupported information, particularly under long-context or multi-turn conditions; the benchmark is intended to measure these failure modes rather than endorse deployment of the evaluated systems in high-stakes settings. Results should therefore not be interpreted as evidence that any evaluated sys-tem is suitable for autonomous use in domains such as healthcare, finance, legal services, or regulatory decision-making without additional safeguards and human oversight.

A Related Work 

Real-Time Voice Agent Benchmarks. Recent benchmarks for full-duplex voice agents pri-marily evaluate conversational dynamics. The 

Full-Duplex-Bench series measures turn-taking, pause and interruption handling (Lin et al., 2025), overlapping speech and backchanneling (Lin et al., 2026c), multi-turn instruction following with an automated examiner (Lin et al., 2026b), and multi-step tool use under naturalistic disfluencies (Lin et al., 2026a). MTR-DuplexBench further evaluate dialogue quality, instruction following, safety, and consistency across extended interactions (He et al., 2026). While these benchmarks provide critical frameworks for auditing the temporal fluidness and conversational mechanics of full-duplex models, they do not measure a model’s fidelity to exter-nal, long-context knowledge sources or character-ize the acoustic-modality degradation that occurs under heavy contextual load. Our proposed DSB-DG complements this line of work by evaluating whether full-duplex agents can retrieve and pre-serve document-grounded information while main-taining real-time spoken interaction. 

Spoken Question Answering. Prior work at the intersection of speech processing and information retrieval has primarily focused on extractive spo-ken question answering (SQA). Datasets such as SpokenSQuAD (Li et al., 2018) and its derivatives evaluate a system’s capacity to locate exact answer spans within audio documents or automated tran-scriptions. However, these resources are fundamen-tally limited to single-turn, short-context scenarios, typically bounding the target audio to brief para-graphs or isolated audio segments. They do not account for multi-turn dialogue drift, long-range structural dependencies, or the open-weight full-duplex architectures that process continuous audio tokens natively. 

Long-Context Document Grounding and RAG. 

Long-context NLP benchmarks including QuAL-ITY (Pang et al., 2022), SCROLLS (Shaham et al., 2022), and LongBench (Bai et al., 2024) evaluate reasoning and information retrieval over extended textual contexts. Subsequent work has character-ized specific long-context failure modes, including “lost in the middle” effects (Liu et al., 2024) and degradation under hard negatives (Jin et al., 2025), while benchmarks such as Long 2RAG (Qi et al., 2024) and M-LongDoc (Chia et al., 2025) extend evaluation to long-form retrieval and multimodal documents. These benchmarks provide strong foun-dations for studying long-context grounding, but assume static text-based interaction and therefore do not capture the latency, conversational drift, or grounding persistence of real-time speech agents. DSB-DG lies at the intersection of these research directions. Unlike full-duplex benchmarks, it eval-uates factual fidelity to external documents; un-like SQA, it targets long-context, generative, multi-turn interaction; and unlike text-based long-context benchmarks, it evaluates grounding within a real-time voice agents loop. Its three protocols further separate failures caused by increasing document length, conversational forgetting, and context re-injection, enabling controlled analysis of document grounding in full-duplex speech systems. 

B Dataset and Benchmark Construction 

B.1 Document Sources and Selection 

DSB-DG contains 50 professional documents, with 10 documents from each of five domains: clinical trial protocols, earnings-call transcripts, FDA 510(k) filings, commercial lease agreements, and municipal meeting minutes. Documents were obtained from the following public sources clinical trial protocols from the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 (clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies ); earnings-call transcripts from SEC EDGAR full-text search ( efts.sec.gov ); FDA 510(k) clearance summaries from the openFDA device API ( api.fda.gov/device/510k.json ); munic-ipal meeting minutes from the Legistar WebAPI for seven U.S. cities (Chicago, Seattle, Boston, Denver, Phoenix, Minneapolis, Portland); and commercial lease agreements that are synthetically generated (no external source). Documents were selected according to per-domain retrieval filters with a target of 10 documents each: sector/SIC quotas (e.g. five clinical sub-areas at 2 docs each; five FDA device sectors at 2 each), date windows (clinical from 2024-01-01; earnings and FDA 2024-01-01 to 2026-03-31; municipal from 2024-06-01), completed-study/decision-code status filters (clinical status=COMPLETED ; FDA 

decision_code=SESE ), and keyword/length filters (municipal requires deliberative keywords such as “motion/vote/ayes/nays/resolution/approved” and 

≥2000 characters) and excluded when a document failed these filters. For earnings, a fixed set of mega-cap tickers was explicitly excluded (AAPL, GOOGL, MSFT, AMZN, META, NVDA, TSLA). Table 8 summarizes the provenance and basic statistics of the corpus. 

B.2 Document Preprocessing 

Raw documents are converted to text using domain-specific parsers that retrieve structured text directly from each provider API (ClinicalTrials.gov, SEC EDGAR, openFDA, Legistar) or, for commercial leases, generate the text programmatically; no PDF rendering is involved. We remove provider API/-markup boilerplate during parsing while preserving the narrative document body used for QA genera-tion. No OCR was required (all sources provide machine-readable text). No document content is summarized or reordered before context-tier con-struction. 

B.3 Context-Tier Construction 

Documents are tokenized using the cl100k_base 

encoding ( tiktoken 0.7.0). We construct five tar-get context tiers of approximately 500, 1k, 2k, 4k, and 8k tokens using a content-preserving budget allocation. The tiers are prefix-consistent such that information present at tier Ti remains available in all subsequent tiers. No text is reordered or sum-marized between tiers. 

B.4 QA Generation 

Candidate question–answer pairs are generated us-ing GPT-OSS-20B. The generation model receives only the plain text of the single assigned tiered document (no metadata, no other tiers) to pre-vent access to information outside the assigned source context. Generation uses temperature 0.7, maximum output length 4096 tokens, and a fixed JSON-only output contract. For each document-tier pair, we generate 12 candidates. Candidate questions target the following reasoning categories: 

entity retrieval , numerical retrieval , procedural rea-soning , clause interpretation , and multi-hop doc-ument comprehension . The question taxonomy is encoded as grounded (subtypes direct_lookup ,

multi_span , inferential ) and unanswerable (subtypes adjacent , temporal , overshoot ). The reasoning categories are classified as entity, numer-ical, clause, procedural, multi-hop).

E.1 Context Saturation 

Each evaluation instance is an independent single-turn interaction. For a QA pair eligible at tier Ti,the identical spoken question is evaluated at every applicable context tier while conversation state is reset between runs. 

Eligibility. A question first becomes eligible at the single tier it was generated from — each QA pair is generated against one specific tiered docu-ment and is evaluated only at that tier. Across all five tiers, no questions are shared across every tier, while each question is evaluated at every tier from its minimum eligible tier through T5. The num-ber of QA whose minimum eligible tier is T1–T5

F Additional Results 

Hallucinations. Table 19 decomposes the Hallu-cination Rate into intrinsic (contradicting the docu-ment) and extrinsic (unsupported additions) errors, and adds the false-confidence rate and the normal-ized partial-credit score (0–2). Across every model, grounding errors are dominated by intrinsic hal-lucinations (contradicting the supplied document) rather than extrinsic ones (inventing outside facts). Models are not “making things up” so much as mis-reading the document in front of them , and they do so confidently — the false-confidence rate tracks HR closely. Gemini-Live is no exception (10.0% in-trinsic vs. 2.6% extrinsic). This argues that ground-ing interventions should target document compre-hension/attention , not external-knowledge suppres-sion. 

Abstention on Unanswerable Questions. The strong systems refuse unanswerable questions well (GPT-Realtime 86.6%, Gemini-Live 85.6%, Cas-caded 84.6%, UV-32B 80.3%, MiniCPM-o 77.2%), whereas SALMONN answers anyway roughly 38% of the time, often with false confidence (FCR 31.4%), and Moshi almost never abstains (NRR 12.5%, FCR 85.7%). H Detailed Latency Analysis 

H.1 FTED Measurement 

First-Token Emission Delay (FTED) is measured from the end of the user audio segment to the first emitted model audio unit. Timing is recorded us-ing wall-clock timers in the streaming evaluation harness. Measurements include network transit time, include audio encoding/decoding, and ex-clude queuing or connection-establishment over-head. Persistent API sessions are not used for Con-text Saturation protocol — each turn opens a fresh stateless session. We report p50, mean, and p95 across turns. 

Latency Measurement Environment. Open-weight latency measurements are collected on NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs under CUDA 12.x, using each model’s native PyTorch/trans-formers inference stack (per-model virtual envi-ronments). To reduce transient network effects, models/servers are loaded once and kept warm and turns run sequentially; Whisper-large-v3 ASR of the model’s spoken response is run on GPU where a card is free (otherwise CPU) and is not counted in FTED.
