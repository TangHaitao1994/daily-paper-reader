Abstract 

High-quality datasets are essential for reliable machine learn-ing, but dataset curation remains costly and hard to generalize across domains. Existing methods typically rely on manually designed heuristics or model-dependent signals, limiting their applicability across tasks and user queries. To address these limitations and automate data curation, we propose Curator-MAS , a multi-agent collaboration framework that orchestrates agents to evaluate and curate high-quality datasets. To achieve the goal of flexible curation, CuratorMAS decomposes the complex curation process into five programmable execution stages and forms a parallelizable workflow. Specifically, Cura-torMAS first performs dataset exploration to collect contextual information such as file structures and constraint cues, thereby developing a comprehensive understanding of the given task. In order to acquire up-to-date information, CuratorMAS re-trieves domain knowledge from online sources to augment the evaluation process. Next, CuratorMAS derives the necessary evaluation criteria and computes the corresponding metrics. Based on these results, CuratorMAS executes filtering accord-ingly. Finally, an evolution module summarizes the evaluation outcomes and updates the relevant skills. Extensive and com-prehensive experiments demonstrate that CuratorMAS signif-icantly reduces the noise rate by up to 36.03 percentage points (pp) while also improving the F1 score of downstream models by up to 8.88 pp. 

# Introduction 

High-quality data are fundamental to the success of mod-ern machine learning systems, but dataset curation remains costly, domain-specific, and difficult to scale (Penedo et al. 2024; Soldaini et al. 2024). As training datasets are increas-ingly collected from web sources, crowdsourced annotations, and synthetically generated samples, dataset curation faces three key challenges: (i) how to automatically construct task-specific datasets; (ii) how to ensure the quality of the curated datasets; and (iii) how to satisfy diverse user requirements. Failure to address these challenges can hinder downstream model training by slowing convergence, amplifying annota-tion bias, and degrading generalization performance. Large-scale data curation pipelines typically rely on pre-defined heuristic rules, such as text-length thresholds and toxicity filters (Penedo et al. 2024; Soldaini et al. 2024; Raf-fel et al. 2020). Another line of work estimates data quality via model-dependent signals, including training dynamics, confident learning, and meta-learned data valuation (North-cutt, Jiang, and Chuang 2021; Li, Socher, and Hoi 2020; Xiao et al. 2023; Qiao et al. 2022; Chong, Hong, and Man-ning 2022; Ethayarajh, Choi, and Swayamdipta 2022; Calian et al. 2025). While these methods provide useful estimates of data quality, they often require task-specific model train-ing or access to model internals, limiting their applicability to diverse user-provided datasets. Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong capa-bilities in reasoning, tool use, and data analysis (Zhu et al. 2026; Qiao et al. 2026), making LLM-based evaluation a promising alternative (Zhang et al. 2023; Lin et al. 2025). Nevertheless, a central question remains: How can agents automatically curate and evaluate datasets across diverse domains and user requirements? 

To this end, we propose CuratorMAS , an automated multi-agent collaboration framework that orchestrates four core curation agents—an Explorer Agent, a Researcher Agent, an Evaluator Agent, and a Filter Agent—to curate and evaluate high-quality datasets. Given a dataset and a user query, CuratorMAS first performs dataset comprehen-sion to analyze the dataset structure and identify constraints imposed by downstream models and user preferences. It then conducts knowledge-augmented quality evaluation, during which the researcher retrieves up-to-date domain knowledge to enrich the system’s evaluation skills, while the evaluator derives appropriate quality criteria and computes the corre-sponding metrics. Based on the results, the filter agent makes criterion-aware filtering decisions and finally produces a cu-rated dataset for downstream use. Unlike static rule-based and model-dependent approaches, CuratorMAS adapts its evaluation criteria to different datasets while providing trans-parent and auditable evaluation results. In addition, its evolu-tion module enables the system to continuously incorporate up-to-date knowledge across runs. Our primary contributions are as follows: (i) We explicitly break down the data curation process into an executable and programmable workflow, providing a structured paradigm for automated data curation. (iii) We evaluate CuratorMAS against six baselines on two datasets spanning different modalities and domains, including rule-based, model-based, and prompt-based meth-ods. CuratorMAS reduces the noise rate by up to 36.03 pp, while improving the F1 scores of the downstream models by up to 8.9 pp through our effective workflow. 

# Related Work 

Dataset Curation. Large-scale dataset construction typi-cally relies on computationally efficient heuristics, such as length and toxicity filtering (Penedo et al. 2024; Soldaini et al. 2024; Raffel et al. 2020), but these rules are dataset-specific and require manual redesign across modalities, tasks, and user needs. Model-based techniques, including confident learning, influence-based valuation, and meta-learned util-ity estimation (Swayamdipta et al. 2020; Northcutt, Jiang, and Chuang 2021; Ethayarajh, Choi, and Swayamdipta 2022; Calian et al. 2025), provide quality estimates but are often tied to specific task domains and are difficult to generalize. Building on recent work using multimodal LLMs for quality assessment (Lin et al. 2025), our framework employs LLMs for query- and domain-aware dataset evaluation and curation. 

Multi-Agent Collaboration. Prior studies on multi-agent systems have demonstrated the benefits of role specialization, debate, and workflow decomposition. Role-playing frame-works assign agents distinct responsibilities for collabora-tive reasoning (Li et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2025), while debate-based methods can improve factuality by enabling agents to critique one another (Du et al. 2024). Engineering-oriented frameworks such as AutoGen (Wu et al. 2023), MetaGPT (Hong et al. 2024), and ChatDev (Qian et al. 2024) support the decomposition of complex tasks into workflows. CuratorMAS extends these principles to dataset curation by decomposing the curation process into specialized stages co-ordinated through a structured multi-agent workflow. 

Self-Evolution. Agent self-evolution enables agents to im-prove by transforming experience into persistent memories, reusable skills, or executable tools. Reflexion (Shinn et al. 2023) stores linguistic feedback in episodic memory, while ExpeL (Zhao et al. 2024) distills reusable insights from past trajectories. Recent studies have explored skill evolu-tion, long-term memory, and the automated construction of reusable tools (Qiu et al. 2025b; Xia et al. 2026; Qiu et al. 2025a). Unlike prior work on general reasoning and program-ming, CuratorMAS applies self-evolution to dataset curation by refining domain skills and developing reusable tools. 

# CuratorMAS 

In this section, we present CuratorMAS, a multi-agent collab-oration framework for curating and evaluating high-quality datasets, as illustrated in Figure 1. CuratorMAS organizes its five-stage workflow into three core components. 1) Dataset Comprehension : Given an input dataset and a user query, the Explorer Agent analyzes the dataset structure, summarizes its key characteristics, and decomposes the user query into constraints such as model requirements and user preferences. 2) Knowledge-Augmented Evaluation : In this open-domain setting, the Researcher Agent identifies gaps in the evalu-ation criteria of the current domain skill and retrieves up-to-date domain knowledge from online sources to augment the evaluation. Subsequently, the Evaluator Agent performs multi-granularity evaluation, after which the Filter Agent fil-ters noisy samples accordingly to produce a curated dataset. 3) Experience-Based Self-Evolution : Based on the evalua-tion results, the Refiner Agent summarizes the evaluation outcomes and updates the domain skills by refining the cri-teria and distilling usable scripts. The overall workflow of CuratorMAS is formalized in Algorithm 1. 

Dataset Comprehension 

To handle dataset heterogeneity across modalities and do-mains, we first employ an Explorer Agent πexplorer to de-velop a comprehensive understanding of the target dataset D.The Explorer Agent analyzes the dataset’s structure, parses data files, and performs essential preprocessing. It then sum-marizes the dataset into a structured document UD , which contains a structural description SD , a sample overview 

OD , and a data-loading protocol LD . This document fur-ther provides guidance for subsequent agent collaboration. The Explorer Agent also decomposes the user query q into model constraints Cmodel and data preferences Rdata . To-gether, these components define the evaluation constraint set 

Cq = {Cmodel , R data }, which bounds the retrieval and eval-uation procedures. By providing downstream agents with a shared representation of the dataset and user requirements, this comprehension stage promotes consistent reasoning and reduces the context overhead caused by repeated exploration.

# Experiments 

Experimental Settings 

Datasets. We evaluate CuratorMAS on three datasets across the image and text modalities. The text benchmarks are (i) JD: a sentiment classification dataset of product reviews from the JD platform (DAMO_NLP 2022). We selected and manually re-annotated 1,000 samples to construct a clean subset, denoted as JD_clean; (ii) NoisyAG-News: a human-annotated news-topic classification dataset with naturally oc-curring label noise (Huang et al. 2024). We selected 3,000 samples with a noise rate of 19.83%. The image benchmark is (iii) CIFAR-10N: a human-annotated image classification dataset containing real-world label noise (Wei et al. 2022). We selected 3,000 samples with a noise rate of 39.67%. 

Baselines. We compare CuratorMAS with six base-lines across three categories: rule-based, model-based, and prompt-based approaches. For the rule-based method, fol-lowing previous work (Penedo et al. 2024; Soldaini et al. 2024; Raffel et al. 2020), we implement (i) Heuristic Rules, which comprises text-integrity checks, anomalous-image-resolution checks, and other heuristics. Model-based meth-ods include (ii) CleanLab (Northcutt, Jiang, and Chuang 2021), which uses confident learning to detect label noise; (iii) DivideMix (Li, Socher, and Hoi 2020), which models the distribution of training losses to distinguish clean sam-ples from noisy ones; (iv) ProMix (Xiao et al. 2023), which combines small-loss selection, pseudo-label expansion, and mixed-sample augmentation; and (v) LossRank (Chong, Hong, and Manning 2022), which leverages loss as an indi-cator. For the prompt-based method, we evaluate (vi) LLM-as-a-Judge, which uses GPT-5.4 (OpenAI 2026b) as a judge to classify whether a sample is noisy.

Controlled Contamination 

We synthetically inject five predefined noise types into JD_clean at varying contamination levels. Specifically, we consider five representative types of noise that may arise dur-ing data collection and annotation: (i) Label Flipping , where the original label is changed to the opposite class; (ii) Off-Domain , where the original review is replaced with irrelevant text from an unrelated corpus, such as THUCNews (Sun et al. 2016); (iii) Ambiguous , where an LLM rewrites the review to make its sentiment ambiguous; (iv) Broken Text , where the review is corrupted by encoding errors, HTML residues, or other textual corruption; and (v) Duplication , where a randomly selected review is duplicated to replace another sample. These noise types are injected in equal proportions to produce overall noise rates of 10%, 20%, and 30%. For each level, we perform a single filtering pass. As shown in Table 1, CuratorMAS achieves favorable per-formance across noise levels while maintaining a low false-positive rate (FPR). At the 30% level, CuratorMAS achieves a recall of 83.00% and a precision of 92.72%, with a rela-tively low FPR of 3.44%. As the noise increases from 10% to 30%, CuratorMAS remains effective, with the F1 score rising from 78.22% to 87.13%, whereas the FPR increases only moderately from 2.80% to 3.44%. This trend may be at-tributed to the increased prevalence of noisy samples, which provides more detectable positive instances. These results in-dicate that the programmable curation workflow can identify and filter multiple types of noise across noise levels.

We apply two rounds of filtering to CIFAR-10N and one round to NoisyAG-News. As shown in Table 2, CuratorMAS achieves the best overall performance among all evaluated methods. On CIFAR-10N, CuratorMAS reaches 95.55% recall, a 7.73-point improvement over the prompt-based method, while maintaining a precision of 73.69% and reducing the residual noise rate to 3.64%. Ablation Study 

Sensitivity Analysis. We conduct a sensitivity analysis us-ing three different LLM backbones for our task-execution agents. Table 3 reveals noticeable performance differences among the three models. GPT-5.4 delivers the best overall performance, achieving a favorable trade-off among preci-sion, recall, and residual noise rate. GPT-5.3-Codex does not outperform either GPT-5.4 or Sonnet-4.6. This result sug-gests that specialization for coding alone does not necessar-ily translate into stronger data-curation capabilities, whereas general reasoning ability may play a more fundamental role in data curation. This observation is broadly consistent with previous research (Zhu et al. 2026). These findings indicate that although CuratorMAS can effectively support automated data curation, its curation performance remains sensitive to the capabilities of the underlying LLM backbone.

# Conclusion 

We presented CuratorMAS, a multi-agent collaboration framework that enables agents to flexibly evaluate and curate datasets across diverse domains. By breaking down the com-plex data curation process into executable and programmable stages, CuratorMAS can adapt its evaluation criteria to dif-ferent domains and dynamic user queries, thereby providing a structured paradigm for automated data curation. Exper-iments on three datasets against six baselines demonstrate the effectiveness of CuratorMAS, which reduces the noise rate by up to 36.03 pp. Moreover, models trained on the cu-rated datasets achieve improvements of up to 8.9 pp in F1 score. These results establish CuratorMAS as a practical and flexible solution to automated data curation across diverse real-world scenarios. Future work includes extending Cura-torMAS to more complex tasks while further improving its generalization and stability for high-stakes applications.
